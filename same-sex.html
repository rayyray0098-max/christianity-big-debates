2. Are same-sex relationships sinful?

Introduction

Christians disagree about whether same-sex relationships are sinful. The disagreement often concerns how a small number of biblical passages should be interpreted, what those passages meant in their original historical setting, and how they should be applied today. Christians on both sides may say that they are trying to follow the Bible faithfully.

Arguments that same-sex relationships are sinful

  • Biblical prohibitions: Some Christians understand Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13 as clear prohibitions of sexual relationships between men.
  • New Testament teaching: Some interpret Romans 1:26–27 and 1 Corinthians 6:9–11 as confirming that same-sex sexual behaviour is sinful.
  • Traditional understanding of marriage: Many churches teach that marriage is a covenant between a man and a woman, often referring to Genesis 2:24 and Jesus' words in Matthew 19:4–6.

Arguments that committed same-sex relationships are not sinful

  • Historical context: Some Christians argue that the biblical passages address specific practices in the ancient world, such as exploitation, abuse or idolatrous behaviour, rather than loving, faithful same-sex partnerships as understood today.
  • Love and faithfulness: They argue that the Bible's wider teachings about love, commitment, justice and faithfulness should guide how relationships are judged.
  • Human dignity: They point to the belief that every person is made in God's image and should be treated with respect, not rejected or mistreated.

Biblical references

  • Leviticus 18:22: A passage traditionally understood by many Christians as prohibiting male same-sex sexual activity.
  • Romans 1:26–27: Paul refers to same-sex behaviour in a discussion of human wrongdoing. Christians disagree about the scope and meaning of this passage.
  • 1 Corinthians 6:9–11: Paul lists behaviours associated with wrongdoing and reminds the church that people can be changed. The translation and meaning of some terms in this passage are debated.
  • Matthew 22:37–40: Jesus identifies love of God and love of neighbour as the greatest commandments.
  • Genesis 1:27: Humanity is described as created in the image of God.

Conclusion

Traditional and affirming Christians interpret some of the same passages differently. The traditional view sees the Bible as restricting sexual relationships to marriage between a man and a woman. The affirming view argues that the passages do not directly address modern, committed same-sex partnerships. The debate involves biblical interpretation, history, church tradition and how Christians understand love and marriage.
