3. Faith vs works — how are we saved?

Introduction

Christians generally believe that salvation is God's gift, but they disagree about how faith and good actions relate to being saved. Some traditions emphasise that people are justified by faith apart from works. Others stress that genuine faith must be active through love and obedience. The disagreement is not always as simple as “faith or good deeds”, because many Christians believe both matter but explain their roles differently.

Arguments for salvation through faith, not earned by works

  • Salvation is a gift: Ephesians 2:8–9 says people are saved by grace through faith, not by works, so nobody can boast.
  • Jesus' sacrifice: Christians who hold this view argue that salvation depends on what Jesus has done, not on people earning God's acceptance through good behaviour.
  • Faith is central: Romans 3:28 says a person is justified by faith apart from works of the law.

Arguments that good works are necessary evidence of faith

  • Faith without works is dead: James 2:17 teaches that faith without actions is dead. Supporters argue that real faith changes how a person lives.
  • Faith works through love: Galatians 5:6 describes faith expressing itself through love.
  • Judgement and actions: Matthew 25:31–46 describes judgement in connection with how people treated those in need.

Biblical references

  • Ephesians 2:8–10: Salvation is by grace through faith, not earned by works; verse 10 also says believers are created to do good works.
  • Romans 3:28: Paul teaches justification by faith apart from works of the law.
  • James 2:14–26: James argues that faith without works is dead and uses Abraham as an example.
  • Galatians 5:6: Faith expresses itself through love.
  • Matthew 25:31–46: Jesus describes the importance of caring for people in need.

Conclusion

A useful way to understand the debate is to ask whether good works are the cause of salvation, or the result and evidence of genuine faith. Many Protestant traditions teach that people are saved by grace through faith and that good works follow as a response. Catholic and other Christian traditions also teach that salvation begins with God's grace, while emphasising that faith must be lived out through love and cooperation with grace. It is inaccurate to say that all Christians believe people can simply earn salvation by being good.
